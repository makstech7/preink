# PreInk can own the missing middle

New US permanent makeup (PMU) artists are mostly short on business confidence, not technical skill: they are afraid of underpricing, struggle to get their first steady clients, do every job alone, and are unsure how to enforce deposits. Their paperwork is a patchwork of DIY Jotform, Google Forms and $3 Etsy Canva files. The products sold to them split into three groups, and each group fails new artists in a different way. Etsy form bundles are cheap and easy to edit, but they are generic, Canva-dependent and thinly reviewed. Consent apps cost $10 to $60 or more per month, and the cheap PMU-specific ones get complaints about failing while the client is already in the chair. Business coaching costs $5,600 to $15,000 and draws refund and value complaints. Nobody serves the middle well: a one-time, US-worded, branded, print-ready set of forms, policies and launch tools with honest, dated state labeling. That is PreInk's opening. It succeeds only if PreInk (1) makes the $29 to $79 price obviously worth more than a $3 Etsy bundle, (2) reaches artists through trainers at the moment they get certified, (3) never claims legal protection it cannot prove, and (4) sets up refunds, download logs and sales tax before launch. The evidence has real limits: Reddit and Facebook groups could not be read, Etsy listing pages were blocked, and many figures come from vendor blogs or search snippets. Read the conclusions as strong hypotheses to check with your wife and her peers, not as settled facts.

## Fear of pricing and empty calendars hurts more than paperwork

The most consistent artist-voiced evidence comes from the Haus of PMU podcast, run by working US artists. Its 2024 to 2025 episodes cover "The Pricing Problem in PMU" (artists "afraid to raise your rates" and "still charging like it's 2018"), slow booking seasons, a business checklist built around "wearing all the hats," burnout, early-career fear, and an artist who lost her business Instagram and Facebook to a hack, which shows the danger of relying only on social media for clients ([Haus of PMU RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2359089.rss)). Academy blogs add that beginners copy local prices without working out their own costs and forget card processing fees ([Moda Donna PMU Academy](https://modadonnabeauty.com/academy-news/ten-the-most-common-mistakes-permanent-makeup-beginners-make-moda-donna-pmu-beauty-academy-recommendations/)). They also note that many beginners offer **"model rates" at 50 to 75% off** to build a portfolio ([Glow Journal](https://glowjournal.pages.dev/beauty-careers/permanent-makeup-training)).

Deposits and no-shows are the **best-documented operational pain**. A PMU industry guide warns that if a client disputes a "deposit" for a service that never happened, "the bank will likely side with the client." It recommends calling it a "non-refundable booking fee" of about 30%, with a 48 to 72 hour notice window and one free reschedule. It also observes that new artists hesitate to enforce their own policies ([PMU Hub](https://www.pmuhub.com/how-to-set-up-a-fair-cancellation-policy-pmu-salons/)). A salon-software blog adds that without recorded acceptance of the policy (date and IP address), "you will lose the dispute" ([Sicus Media](https://www.sicusmedia.com/blog/salon-deposit-policy-template.html)). Neither source is a lawyer or a card network, so this is industry practice, not law. It is still the most concrete, fixable pain in the research, and PreInk's policy documents can address it directly.

Training leaves a business gap that academies themselves monetize. One academy sells a separate 90-day coaching program for graduates of 2-day courses who start "without having a concrete plan" ([Huemon Beauty](https://huemonbeautytrainings.my.canva.site)). Another offers an optional mentorship to help students build their business ([Elite PMU](https://elitepmu.com/?p=9487)). Training already costs about **$1,500 to $4,500 for a microblading course and $5,000 to $10,000 or more for full programs** ([Elite PMU](https://elitepmu.com/permanent-makeup-school-cost/)). A microblading session sells for roughly **$400 to $900** ([The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/eyebrow-microblading-pros-and-cons)). Against that, a $49 kit costs less than a tenth of one booking. That is an easy return-on-investment story, provided the artist believes the kit gets her that booking or saves her one no-show.

Licensing confusion and insurance come up less often but steadily. Rules differ by state and often by county, and schools' summaries of them conflict ([Hyper Real Academy](https://hyperrealacademy.com/pmu-licensing-requirements-by-state/); [Vagaro](https://www.vagaro.com/learn/license-to-microblade)). Insurers point to clients who claim results "look nothing like" what they expected ([Allied Beauty Experts](https://alliedbeautyexperts.com/microblading-liability-insurance/)). One caution: the counts of how many sources raised each theme come from about 20 mostly commercial sources, not from forum volume. The ranking of pains is therefore directional.

## Buyers praise easy editing and punish anything that fails at appointment time

The table below summarizes what each existing product type offers and where it falls short for a brand-new solo artist.

| Category | Typical price | What buyers praise | What buyers complain about or what is missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etsy PMU form bundles (Canva/Word/PDF) | ~$2 to $10, "mega" bundles up to ~$31 | "Very easy to edit in Canva"; "everything and more" | One 1-star review: could not print as delivered; most listings have very few reviews; generic, no state labeling |
| Indie "attorney reviewed" forms | $6 (MN, Word) to $150 (CA, one waiver) | Legal-review claim lets the seller charge about 25x Etsy prices | Heavy disclaimers undercut the claim; one state only; all sales final |
| UK business bundle (Hannce) | £47 | Forms plus 5 policies plus communication pack | UK product; personal-use license |
| PMU consent apps (PMU Forms, REV23, Microblading App) | ~$120 to $240/yr, or pay per form | Fast setup, pre-appointment forms, locked signatures, photo storage | "Stops working of course when I need it most"; lost client data; uneven ratings across iOS and Android |
| Booking suites (GlossGenius, Vagaro, Square) | Forms need $48 to $56/mo tiers or add-ons | All-in-one | Forms locked behind upper tiers or sold as add-ons; fees "stack up" |
| UK aesthetics software (Pabau, Consentz, Faces) | $59 to $62+/mo, or free | Onboarding, template counts | Glitches, unclear pricing, injectable-focused templates, GDPR framing rather than US |
| Business coaching | $5,600 to $15,000 | Concrete pricing and marketing systems | Refund disputes, "no price transparency," buyer regret |

Sources for the table: Etsy pricing and review snippets ([Etsy market: microblading forms](https://www.etsy.com/market/microblading_forms); [Etsy 1332625107](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1332625107/editable-pmu-consent-form-template); [Etsy 1559027316](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1559027316/esthetician-consent-forms-forms-editable)); [Outer Beauty PMU](https://www.outerbeautypmu.com/shop/p/permanent-makeup-intake-consent-form-template-with-aftercare-easy-editable-pmu-word-document-microblading-lip-blushing-eyeliner-tattoo); [Seduire Essentials](https://www.seduiressentials.com/product-page/semi-permanent-makeup-waiver-release-form); [Hannce](https://hannce.samcart.com/products/pmu-business-bundle); [PMU Forms on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pmu-forms/id1497270923); [REV23 pricing](https://www.rev23.com/pricing/); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.workinprogress.microblading); [GlossGenius pricing](https://glossgenius.com/pricing); [Pabau blog](https://pabau.com/blog/best-aesthetic-clinic-software/); [Software Advice on Consentz](https://www.softwareadvice.com/medical/consentz-profile/reviews/); [Trustpilot PMU Launch](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pmulaunch.com); [Brows by Linnie](https://www.schedulista.com/schedule/browsbylinnie/b6otx-1vfnr).

Across all of these, praise goes to **speed and ease** (edit in Canva in minutes, set up an app in under five minutes) and to **completeness** (one bundle that has "everything"). The sharpest complaints concern **failure at the worst moment**: a PMU Forms user wrote that the app kept buffering and lost access to client data right before an appointment ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pmu-forms/id1497270923)), and a Faces Consent user abandoned the app because features "never work" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/faces-consent/id1467896114)). The second complaint theme is **subscription creep**. GlossGenius puts "Forms & Waivers" in its $56/month Gold tier rather than its $28 entry plan ([GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/pricing)), and Jotform's HIPAA features start at $99/month ([Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/jotform-pricing-2026-plans-costs-what-you-get)). The third is the **printing and format friction** of Canva-only files: the one surfaced 1-star Etsy review was about printing, and sellers add how-to-edit videos to compensate.

The research did *not* find direct buyer complaints about typos, UK spelling, generic wording or missing state rules. Those are reasonable guesses based on how listings are built, but no review says so. Etsy review text was unreachable, so the honest conclusion is that complaints are rare in what could be seen, partly because review counts are tiny. At $3, unhappy buyers probably just stop using the forms instead of complaining.

The business-guide tier is thin. Tina Davies' "Ink to Income" guide is the closest US analogue, with form templates, a list of state licensing boards and a startup checklist, but its price could not be read ([tinadavies.com](https://tinadavies.com/products/ink-to-income-the-business-setup-guide-by-carla-ricciardone)). Above it sit coaching programs, where a PMU Launch reviewer says a refund promise was broken on a $5,600 program and another wished they had "gone with my gut" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pmulaunch.com)).

## Four gaps nobody serves well

**First, the missing middle tier.** Buyers can choose $3 forms with no business help, or thousands of dollars of coaching. No concrete US "business setup kit" was found between roughly $25 and $100 that combines forms, policies, pricing tools and a client-acquisition plan. The closest comparable is a UK £47 bundle with forms and policies but no pricing calculator or first-clients plan ([Hannce](https://hannce.samcart.com/products/pmu-business-bundle)).

**Second, honest state awareness.** No product was found that claims coverage of all 50 states or offers per-state versions. The premium sellers that exist anchor on a single state's attorney review and then disclaim it heavily. Seduire says its forms were "reviewed by California licensed attorneys" while also saying none of its staff are attorneys and the forms come "as-is" ([Seduire Essentials](https://www.seduiressentials.com/product-page/semi-permanent-makeup-waiver-release-form)). State body-art rules on consent and record-keeping do exist, for example in Oklahoma, Utah and Georgia ([OK Admin Code 310:233-3-8](https://regulations.justia.com/states/oklahoma/title-310/chapter-233/subchapter-3/section-310-233-3-8); [Utah R392-701-14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R392-701-14); [Georgia 511-3-8-.08](https://regulations.justia.com/states/georgia/department-511/chapter-511-3/subject-511-3-8/rule-511-3-8-08)). Vendor guides disagree on retention periods, quoting anywhere from 1 to 7 years ([TattooStudioPro](https://tattoostudiopro.pages.dev/checklist-for-tattoo-studio-consent-compliance/)). PreInk's planned rule, "state version only after checking the official source, otherwise a clearly labeled general version," is exactly what is missing. Adding a visible link to the official source and the date it was checked would make the rule credible.

**Third, a policy set built to survive disputes.** Real studio policies include clauses like "client will not initiate a chargeback," which are of doubtful value ([Daisy PMUA](https://daisypmua.com/pages/about-us-terms-and-policies)). Industry guidance says what helps is clear "booking fee" wording, a signed and dated acknowledgment, and consistent enforcement ([PMU Hub](https://www.pmuhub.com/how-to-set-up-a-fair-cancellation-policy-pmu-salons/); [Primzy Beauty](https://www.primzybeauty.com/blog/client-disputes-a-charge-beauty-pros)). No form product markets itself on this.

**Fourth, a reliable paper backup for app users.** Regulators accept paper kept in a locked cabinet ([TattooStudioPro](https://tattoostudiopro.pages.dev/checklist-for-tattoo-studio-consent-compliance/)), and Tina Davies notes that paper has been the PMU tradition ([Tina Davies guide](https://tinadavies.com/pages/the-essential-guide-to-pmu-consent-forms)). Given the "fails when I need it most" complaints, a branded printable set is a natural companion to any app, not only a competitor. The same text can also be pasted into Google Forms or a booking suite later. What a PDF kit cannot match is built-in e-signing and storage, so PreInk should make it easy to pair with free tools rather than compete on that.

## Pricing, channels and launch need to beat a $3 anchor

PreInk's $29 floor is about ten times the typical Etsy bundle, and free hosted templates exist from Paperform and Typeform ([Paperform](https://paperform.co/templates/permanent-makeup-consent-form/); [Typeform](https://www.typeform.com/templates/permanent-makeup-consent-form)). Price is defensible only if the free preview makes the difference obvious in seconds: the artist's own name and colors on a complete, print-ready, multi-service set, with no Canva editing. The better comparison to make is against her own spending (a $3,000 course, a $500 session), not against Etsy. Pricing research on three-tier menus favors the middle option, the compromise effect, so **$49 should be the tier designed to be the obvious pick**. The $79 tier needs visible extras in the preview, such as all five styles, the scripts and the first-ten-clients plan, not just a longer list ([The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/decoy-effect)). That evidence is second-hand and not specific to templates, so test it. The $79 tier is the least supported price in this research: no US comparable at that level was found.

The strongest channel is **trainers and academies**, because they reach artists at the exact "just certified, now opening" moment and already sell business add-ons. They are also competitors, since many hand out their own forms. Typical digital-education affiliate rates of 25 to 30% would earn a trainer only $12 to $24 per sale ([Bloggingtips on Teachable](https://bloggingtips.com/teachable-affiliate-program)), so a co-branded graduate edition or a bulk license may interest them more than a commission. Hannce already charges separately for an "Educator Licence," which shows trainers will pay for that right ([Hannce](https://hannce.samcart.com/products/pmu-business-bundle)). On social media, PMU marketing advice favors short video and process content ([Girlz Ink](https://www.girlzink.com/blogs/news/pmu-marketing-strategies-that-still-work-in-todays-beauty-industry)). A "messy Word doc versus branded kit" before-and-after fits that feed naturally. SPCP's 2026 convention (September, Phoenix) has already passed, but its Approved Supplier and sponsor programs are a route into the next one ([SPCP](https://spcp.org/convention); [SPCP supplier](https://join.spcp.org/supplier)). Podcasts like Haus of PMU, whose topics match PreInk's kit almost item for item, are a natural guest or sponsor target. Selling on Etsy would put PreInk in the $3 price war and cost about 6.5% transaction fees plus 3% + $0.25 processing, plus possible 12 to 15% Offsite Ads fees ([Marmalead](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-fees-explained-2025/)). Use Etsy for discovery at most, not as the main storefront.

Messaging should lead with outcomes artists already voice: charge what you are worth (calculator), fill your book (model call script and first-ten-clients plan), stop losing booking fees (policy plus signed acknowledgment), and look like a real studio from day one (branded set). It should also say plainly "one payment, no subscription, prints when your app doesn't." The time-saving angle matters for people "wearing all the hats."

## Legal, refund, copying and tax risks are manageable if handled up front

**Legal claims and unauthorized practice of law (UPL).** The precedent to know is *Parsons Technology* (Texas, 1999). A court held that selling Quicken Family Lawyer software was unauthorized practice of law, and a disclaimer shown only on first use did not save it. Texas then changed its law to exclude software and similar products that clearly and conspicuously say they are not a substitute for an attorney, and the injunction was vacated ([Texas House Research Org](https://lrl.texas.gov/scanned/hroBillAnalyses/76-0/HB1507.PDF); [Syfert summary](https://syfert.com/caselaw/case.php?id=18064)). LegalZoom has also faced UPL suits over its forms ([Georgetown Journal of Legal Ethics](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/legal-ethics-journal/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2019/11/GT-GJLE190045.pdf)). The takeaway is that general published templates with prominent, repeated disclaimers sit on the safer side. Anything that looks like individualized advice ("which clause should I use for my situation?") moves toward the line. Only one state's rule was researched, so a lawyer should review PreInk's setup.

**Advertising claims.** The FTC fined DoNotPay $193,000 and barred it from claiming lawyer-level performance without evidence ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87474)). For PreInk, words like "legally compliant," "lawyer approved," "covers all 50 states" or "protects you from lawsuits" are claims you would have to prove. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake or AI-generated reviews, review suppression and undisclosed insider reviews ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)). Under the Endorsement Guides, affiliates and anyone with a material connection must disclose it clearly ([FTC FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). That includes your wife: her testimonial must say she is connected to PreInk, and beta testers who got the kit free must say so too.

**Chargebacks and refunds.** Stripe's analysis of a million disputes found that usage or download logs raised digital sellers' win rates by about 10 percentage points. For digital goods, issuing a full refund was the strongest predictor of a good outcome ([Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/analyzing-the-evidence-that-helps-businesses-win-product-not-received-disputes); [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/best-practices)). Etsy form sellers mostly say "all sales final" ([Seduire](https://www.seduiressentials.com/product-page/semi-permanent-makeup-waiver-release-form)). A clear, quick refund promise is both safer and a selling point, because the free preview already filters out most mismatched buyers.

**Copying.** Blank forms "designed for recording information" are not copyrightable, and registration protects only original added expression ([37 CFR 202.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-202/section-202.1); [Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). Expect form text to be copied. What PreInk can defend is its design, the instructional and explanatory writing, the generator and the brand. A personal-use license (as Hannce uses) plus the buyer's name printed on each PDF discourages resale.

**Sales tax.** Taxability of digital products varies by state. Guides count anywhere from 28 to 41 states plus DC, depending on definitions, and Louisiana added digital products in 2025 ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/state-by-state-guide-to-digital-products-and-sales-tax.html); [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/sales-tax/sales-tax-on-digital-goods-by-state/); [Sales.tax](https://sales.tax/expert-articles/sales-tax-on-digital-goods-whats-taxable-in-2026-and-what-changed/)). Do not track this by hand. Use a merchant of record or an automatic tax tool from day one.

## What the evidence cannot tell you

There are **no verbatim Reddit or Facebook artist posts** in this research. Both were unreachable, and they are where new PMU artists actually talk, so the ranking of pains rests on podcast titles, academy blogs and vendor content, all of which have something to sell. **Etsy listing pages returned errors**, so prices, sales counts (a few listings showing roughly 850 to 890 sales) and review snippets are search-result snapshots. One "3,699 reviews" figure is probably shop-wide, not for one listing. Tina Davies' guide price and Hannce's full page could not be read. Vagaro and Square forms pricing comes from third parties that disagree with each other. There is **no reliable count of US PMU artists**, no failure-rate data, no US chargeback or no-show statistics for PMU, and no conversion data on which trust signals sell templates. State record-retention periods come from conflicting vendor blogs, and only Texas UPL law was examined. Your wife's direct access to artist Facebook groups and her peers is the cheapest way to fill the biggest gap.

## Prioritized recommendations for PreInk

1. **Get a short lawyer review of the disclaimer setup and claim wording before launch.** Show "not legal advice, not a substitute for an attorney" on the site, at checkout and on every document, and drop any "compliant," "lawyer approved" or "protects you" language. Evidence: the *Parsons* court discounted a one-time disclaimer, Texas's exemption requires a clear and conspicuous statement, and the FTC acted against DoNotPay.
2. **Ship state versions only with a visible "Official source: [link], checked [date]" line, and label everything else "General US version, check your state."** Make that labeling a headline feature. Evidence: no competitor offers honest multi-state coverage, state rules and retention periods vary and are poorly documented, and premium sellers rely on one-state attorney claims that they then disclaim.
3. **Make the free watermarked preview the main sales tool: the artist's own name, colors and service list rendered instantly, print-ready, with no Canva needed.** Evidence: Etsy buyers praise easy editing but hit printing friction. The preview is also the lowest-risk trust signal under FTC rules, and it is what justifies charging about 10x Etsy.
4. **Build the deposit, cancellation and touch-up policy around "booking fee" wording, a client acknowledgment line and a dated signature. Add a one-page "how to enforce this kindly" guide.** Evidence: this is the best-documented operational pain, and industry guidance says bare "deposit" wording and unrecorded acceptance lose disputes. Present it as best practice, not a guarantee.
5. **Lead the messaging with pricing confidence and filling the calendar, and put the pricing calculator and first-ten-clients plan in the $49 tier.** Evidence: pricing fear and slow bookings are the most-voiced pains, and the middle tier is where most buyers land.
6. **Position the kit as "one payment, no subscription, and the paper backup when your app fails." Include notes on pasting the text into Google Forms or a booking app, and on free e-signing.** Evidence: app reviewers complain about failures mid-appointment and subscription creep, and forms are locked in $48 to $56/month tiers.
7. **Recruit 3 to 5 trainers with a co-branded graduate edition or bulk license, not only an affiliate link.** Evidence: trainers reach artists at the moment of need, already sell business add-ons and pay for educator licenses, and a 25 to 30% commission is only $12 to $24 per sale.
8. **Set up the money side before the first sale: a merchant of record or automatic tax tool, per-buyer download logs (time and IP), a clear refund policy shown at checkout, and fast refunds on request.** Evidence: digital-product tax varies across 28 to 41 states, Stripe data shows that logs and quick refunds are what work in digital-goods disputes, and refund fights are what hurt the coaching sellers.
9. **Protect the design, not the text: a personal-use license, the buyer's name printed on each PDF, and no resale or educator use without a separate license.** Evidence: blank forms are not copyrightable, and competitors rely on license terms.
10. **Run a validation sprint with your wife's network before spending on ads.** Have her collect real quotes from PMU Facebook groups and Reddit on paperwork, pricing and deposits, and have 5 to 10 new artists try the preview. Any testimonials must disclose a free kit or a family connection. Evidence: forum voice is the biggest gap in this research, and the FTC requires disclosure of material connections.
11. **Treat Etsy as a discovery channel at most, and test whether $79 sells before investing more in that tier.** Evidence: Etsy's $3 price anchor and fees (6.5% plus processing plus possible 12 to 15% Offsite Ads) undercut premium positioning, and no US comparable at $79 was found.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of PreInk from "nicer consent forms" to "the missing business-setup layer between a $3 template and a $5,600 coach." Forms alone are a commodity that cannot hold a $29 to $79 price. The bundle of branding, pricing confidence, a booking-fee policy that holds up, and honest dated state labels has no clear US competitor. The same honesty that limits PreInk's marketing claims is also its best differentiator: in a market where competitors say "attorney reviewed" and disclaim it in the next paragraph, "here is exactly what we verified, from which official source, on which date" is both safer and more persuasive.

The biggest open question is not legal or technical. It is whether new artists will pay roughly ten times Etsy prices for a done-for-you result, and the free preview plus your wife's network can answer that cheaply before PreInk spends on growth.

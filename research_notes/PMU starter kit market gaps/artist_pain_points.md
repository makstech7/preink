# PMU Artist Pain Points (US, new and struggling artists)

> **Method note, read first.** Reddit (old.reddit.com and www.reddit.com, including the JSON search endpoint) could not be fetched from this environment, and web search with `site:reddit.com` returned no Reddit threads. Facebook groups are not publicly indexed. So this file has **no verbatim Reddit or Facebook artist quotes**. The closest first-hand "artist voice" here comes from podcast episode descriptions written by working US PMU artists (Haus of PMU, 2024 to 2025) and from studio policy pages. Most other material is academy, vendor or insurer content, which has a commercial interest. Theme frequency below is a count of how many of the ~20 distinct sources reviewed raised each theme. It is not a measure of how often artists actually raise it. A follow-up pass with a logged-in browser on r/PermanentMakeup and r/microblading is the main open gap.

## 1. What do new PMU/microblading artists struggle with most in their first year?

### Takeaway
Across the sources that were reachable, the themes that recur most are underpricing and fear of raising prices, getting enough clients (including slow seasons), "wearing every hat" alone and burning out, technical confidence on early clients, and market oversupply from short courses. Licensing confusion and insurance come up less often, but consistently. No source quantified first-year failure or quit rates.

### Cited Findings
**Theme frequency (sources raising each theme, out of ~20 reviewed):**
- Pricing and underpricing: about 5 sources
- Getting clients, slow bookings and reliance on Instagram: about 5
- Doing everything alone, burnout: about 4
- Licensing and regulatory fragmentation: about 4
- Policies, deposits and no-shows: about 6
- Consent and health-history paperwork: about 3 (plus many form templates)
- Insurance and liability: about 3
- Oversupply and competition: about 2
- Technical confidence and depth errors: about 3

**Getting clients and slow periods**
- A Haus of PMU episode is titled "Slow times in PMU..." and covers "slow times in permanent makeup bookings" (May 2024, US). — [Haus of PMU RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2359089.rss)
- Episode 15, "I Got Hacked: Losing My Business Instagram & Facebook" (May 2025), is about the risk of "relying solely on social media" for clients. — [Haus of PMU RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2359089.rss)
- PMU Marketer Podcast episode #72: an artist describes getting only 2 to 3 clients a week before hitting a $10k month. Another episode covers an artist who struggled to make money in her first year. These were seen only in search-result descriptions, which were not fetched. The host sells marketing services. — [PMU Marketer Podcast (Podtail listing)](https://podtail.com/en/podcast/the-pmu-marketer-podcast/)
- Most PMU artists work solo or rent one room with no front desk, so missed calls lose consultations. This is a vendor claim from a call-answering product. — [Callpad](https://www.callpad.ai/post/microblading-permanent-makeup-studios-missed-calls)
- A school testimonial describes an artist who started in her apartment and grew by word of mouth before renting salon booths. — surfaced via search; source page not fetched, so treat as anecdotal.

**Pricing**

- Many new artists offer "model rates" at 50 to 75% off standard pricing to build a portfolio. — [Glow Journal guide](https://glowjournal.pages.dev/beauty-careers/permanent-makeup-training)

**Doing everything alone, burnout**

- Ep. 14, "From Burnout to Breakthrough" (Dec 2024): "recognize and overcome burnout as a PMU artist." — same source
- An academy blog names "doing everything alone" as a major error that leads to burnout. — [Elite Aesthetics Academy](https://coloradoaestheticsacademy.com/common-mistakes-new-permanent-makeup-artists-make-and-how-to-avoid-them) (from the search summary only)

- Ep. 8, "Small Town, Busy Artist" (Aug 2024): "overcoming those early career fears." Ep. 7, on troubleshooting during procedures: "Permanent makeup can be scary." — [Haus of PMU RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2359089.rss)
- Common beginner errors named include pigment depth that is too deep or too shallow, skipping brow mapping, and choosing pigment by hair colour rather than skin undertone. — [Moda Donna PMU Academy](https://modadonnabeauty.com/academy-news/ten-the-most-common-mistakes-permanent-makeup-beginners-make-moda-donna-pmu-beauty-academy-recommendations/)

**Competition and oversupply**
- A marketing agency says the market is "flooded" with newly certified artists from weekend courses. This is a vendor claim. — [CenterGrowth](https://centergrowth.com/microblading-marketing)
- A Canadian health authority worried that people with no aesthetics background take a one-day course and start working the next day. This source is from Canada and is older, around 2016. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/3080764/interior-health-warning-okanagan-residents-about-a-cosmetic-procedure/amp/)

**Licensing confusion (what sources say, not legal fact)**
- Rules are fragmented. Many states treat PMU as tattooing or body art under health departments, some require a cosmetology or esthetics license, and enforcement often sits with the county. Sources conflict in places. — [Hyper Real Academy](https://hyperrealacademy.com/pmu-licensing-requirements-by-state/); [Vagaro](https://www.vagaro.com/learn/license-to-microblade); [PulsePMU](https://www.pulsepmu.com/post/permanent-makeup-license-usa)
- Elite PMU says California regulates PMU through county body art registration. This is a school source; verify it against the California Department of Public Health. — [Elite PMU](https://elitepmu.com/microblading-license/)
- Haus of PMU Ep. 11 covers the "rigorous Oklahoma requirements" for licensure (Nov 2024). — [Haus of PMU RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2359089.rss)

**Insurance**
- New artists often assume homeowner's or renter's insurance covers their professional equipment. — [Elite Aesthetics Academy](https://coloradoaestheticsacademy.com/common-mistakes-new-permanent-makeup-artists-make-and-how-to-avoid-them) (from the search summary only)
- Insurers frame professional liability as the key cover. An example claim is a client saying the results look nothing like what they expected and alleging the artist is unqualified. — [Allied Beauty Experts](https://alliedbeautyexperts.com/microblading-liability-insurance/); [Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/personal-care-business-insurance/permanent-makeup-artists)

### Inferences
- The kit's pricing calculator and price menu speak directly to the most-voiced emotional pain, fear of undercharging and of raising prices. The first-clients plan and model call script speak to slow bookings and the dependence on Instagram.
- "Wearing every hat" suggests that time saved, with ready-made documents, is a selling point alongside looking professional.

### Gaps
- No verbatim Reddit or Facebook artist posts could be retrieved, so these frequency counts cannot be validated against real forum volume.
- No source gave first-year failure or quit rates for PMU artists.

## 2. Consent forms, waivers, health history, aftercare and policies: where artists get them and what goes wrong

### Takeaway
Artists mostly build their own forms on Jotform, Google Forms or Smartwaiver, or in booking apps, and the wording varies a lot. The forms lean heavily on shifting healing outcomes onto the client's aftercare. Industry content stresses that consent protects the artist for insurance purposes, but it documents few concrete disputes.

### Cited Findings
- **Where forms come from:** Search for PMU consent forms returns dozens of individually built Jotform and Smartwaiver forms from studios. That points to DIY assembly rather than a standard source. — e.g. [Jotform PMU consent](https://form.jotform.com/252100844970150); [Jotform health history](https://form.jotform.com/232886767098073); [Smartwaiver example](https://waiver.smartwaiver.com/v/framedbeautyco)
- Tina Davies (a pigment brand) recommends Google Forms as the simplest option, or the Consult App, which texts forms ahead of the appointment. Forms screen contraindications that can cause "poor results," protect the artist "for insurance purposes," and many insurers require before-and-after photos, so a photo release is useful. The page is undated; its content looks like 2020 to 2021 because of COVID screening references. — [Tina Davies guide](https://tinadavies.com/pages/the-essential-guide-to-pmu-consent-forms)
- **Healing blame language in real forms:** Forms say the artist cannot control the healing process, that touch-ups may cost extra, that the client agrees to follow aftercare to avoid "pigment loss, or poor healing," and that results vary with skin type, age, lifestyle and aftercare. — [Jotform consent forms](https://form.jotform.com/242866132770156); [Formsite PMU form](https://fs3.formsite.com/business_forms/pmu_form2/index.html)
- Some forms use very broad "client assumes full responsibility" clauses. How enforceable they are is untested in any source reviewed. — [Jotform examples](https://form.jotform.com/81940693284869)
- Software vendors such as PmuGo bundle forms with booking, which shows that apps are another source. — [PmuGo](https://pmugo.com/en)
- **Disputes over outcomes:** An insurer's example is a client alleging results "look nothing like" what was expected, plus a negligence claim. — [Allied Beauty Experts](https://alliedbeautyexperts.com/microblading-liability-insurance/)
- In UK data from Zurich (2022), eyebrow microblading and tinting made up 37% of salon injury claims, with microblading alone at 9%. **This is UK data.** — [Insurance Times](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/news/botched-eyebrow-microblading-and-tinting-account-for-37-of-salon-injury-claims-zurich/1441997.article)
- Haus of PMU Ep. 5 covers client skin sensitivity disrupting appointments, which supports the need for pre-care instructions. — [Haus of PMU RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2359089.rss)

### Inferences
- Because forms are scattered across Jotform and Smartwaiver with inconsistent wording, there is room for a coherent, branded set: consent, health history, pre-care, aftercare and photo release that reference each other.
- The kit should avoid promising legal protection, since no source shows these clauses hold up. It can include a "have a local attorney review" note.

### Gaps
- Found no first-hand accounts of artists using Etsy templates or trainer-supplied forms, and nothing on what went wrong with them. Etsy was not searched directly.
- No US data on how often PMU clients dispute results or file claims.

## 3. Training academies leaving out the business side

### Takeaway
Academy marketing itself suggests the gap. Business content is often sold as a separate coaching or mentorship add-on after a 2-to-5-day technical course. No first-hand artist complaints could be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Huemon Beauty sells a separate 90-day coaching programme to give new artists business skills. It describes new artists who took a 2-day course "without having a concrete plan." — [Huemon Beauty](https://huemonbeautytrainings.my.canva.site)
- Elite PMU in Los Angeles offers an optional mentorship in which students "may work towards building their business." — [Elite PMU](https://elitepmu.com/?p=9487)
- Some courses do list business modules, for example "Business strategy in Permanent Makeup," and TLe Beauty's course claims to cover business knowledge. — [Denver program](https://finch-paddlefish-29sa.squarespace.com/permanent-makeup-folder); [Groupon TLe Beauty](https://www.groupon.com/deals/tle-beauty-studio-academy-5)
- An academy argues that many early-career mistakes trace back to inadequate training. — [Moda Donna PMU Academy](https://modadonnabeauty.com/academy-news/ten-the-most-common-mistakes-permanent-makeup-beginners-make-moda-donna-pmu-beauty-academy-recommendations/)

### Inferences
- A paid business add-on, often $1,000 or more in coaching, sits above the kit's likely price point. A low-cost kit can be positioned as "the business binder your training didn't include."

## 4. Deposits, no-shows and chargebacks

### Takeaway
This is the most concretely documented operational pain. Industry advice consistently says a policy labelled "deposit" can lose a chargeback. Artists are told to use a "non-refundable booking fee," get timestamped acceptance, and enforce the policy consistently. New artists are said to hesitate to enforce their policies.

### Cited Findings
- PMU Hub (updated Nov 2025, no country specified) says that if a client disputes a "deposit" for a service that was not performed, "the bank will likely side with the client." It recommends calling it a "non-refundable booking fee," about 30% of the price, with a 48-to-72-hour notice window and one transfer allowed. It notes that new artists hesitate to enforce policies and that repeat cancellers are a red flag. — [PMU Hub](https://www.pmuhub.com/how-to-set-up-a-fair-cancellation-policy-pmu-salons/)
- Without recorded acceptance of the policy (date and IP), "you will lose the dispute." Risk is highest when the policy is buried, vague, or contradicted by what the client was told verbally. — [Sicus Media (2026)](https://www.sicusmedia.com/blog/salon-deposit-policy-template.html)
- Evidence for no-show disputes includes the accepted policy, the booking confirmation, reminders and the cancellation time. — [Primzy Beauty](https://www.primzybeauty.com/blog/client-disputes-a-charge-beauty-pros)
- Real studio policies include a $100 cancellation or no-show fee, treating more than 15 minutes late as a no-show that forfeits the deposit, and clauses saying "client will not initiate a chargeback." The last clause is of doubtful enforceability. — [Desert Bloom Beauty](https://desertbloombeauty.wordpress.com/policies/); [Daisy PMUA](https://daisypmua.com/pages/about-us-terms-and-policies); [Tattoo by Rose](https://www.tattoobyrose.com/terms-of-use)
- Haus of PMU Ep. 4, "Booking Fees: PMU Edition" (May 2024), weighs whether to require booking fees. — [Haus of PMU RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2359089.rss)
- A claim that non-refundable deposits are legal in the US when disclosed comes from a salon blog and was not verified against an official source. — [Sicus Media](https://www.sicusmedia.com/blog/salon-deposit-policy-template.html)

### Inferences
- The deposit, cancellation and touch-up policy in the kit should come with a "client acknowledgment" line or checkbox and a dated signature. Wording that avoids bare "deposit" language is a concrete differentiator.

### Gaps
- No US statistics on PMU no-show rates or chargeback frequency.

## 5. Data points: number of artists, failure rates, prices, training costs

### Takeaway
There is no reliable count of US PMU artists and no quit-rate data. Prices and training costs are available, but only from schools and vendors.

### Cited Findings
- **Headcount:** A career site claims "35K US Jobs," attributed to BLS. There is no PMU-specific BLS category, so this is unreliable. — [USA Career Finder](https://www.usacareerfinder.com/careers/permanent-makeup-artist.html)
- **Microblading price:** An average of just under $600 in the US, ranging from $200 to $2,000. — [PMU Hub salary article](https://www.pmuhub.com/microblading-artist-salary/)
- **Training cost:** Hands-on microblading courses run about $1,500 to $4,500, and full PMU programmes $5,000 to $10,000. One LA school charges $2,500 to $3,000 per 3-to-5-day course and $6,900 to $26,900 for multi-technique programmes. Online courses run about $1,200 to $2,000. — [Elite PMU cost](https://elitepmu.com/permanent-makeup-school-cost/); [Elite PMU microblading price](https://elitepmu.com/microblading-training-price/); [NYIB](https://www.nyib.edu/product/permanent-makeup-and-microblading-course/)
- **Market size:** The global PMU market was about $154M in 2023 and is projected to roughly double by the early 2030s. This is a vendor-cited figure. — [Allied Market Research](https://www.alliedmarketresearch.com/permanent-makeup-market-A325167)
- **Insurance cost:** Vendors cite policies "from ~$37/month." This is marketing and unverified. — [Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/personal-care-business-insurance/permanent-makeup-artists)

### Inferences
- An artist who has just spent $1,500 to $10,000 on training plus supplies is price-sensitive but used to paying for tools. A kit priced well below one microblading service, around $600, is an easy ROI story: one prevented no-show or one extra booking pays for it.

### Gaps
- No US practitioner count from state registries, no attrition or quit-rate data, and no survey of PMU artist income.

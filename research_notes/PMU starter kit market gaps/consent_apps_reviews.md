# Consent Form, Client Intake and Studio Software for PMU / Beauty Artists: Reviews, Pricing, Gaps

Research date: 2026-11-17. Prices are as shown on the pages fetched on or near this date unless labeled otherwise. Reddit could not be searched or fetched (the domain is blocked to this research tool), so practitioner forum sentiment is missing. See Gaps.

## Tools covered: pricing and main features (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
PMU-specific consent apps are cheap ($0-$20/month), but they are small and lightly reviewed, and reliability complaints come up often. All-in-one booking suites put forms in mid or upper tiers ($48-$62+/month), or sell them as add-ons. Generic form builders gate HIPAA features behind their expensive plans ($99+/month). Every option is a subscription except a few free or pay-per-form apps.

### Cited Findings
**PMU-specific apps (US-relevant)**
- **PMU Forms** (Dephyned, iOS + web): free download. In-app subscriptions are $14.99 (Standard, presumably monthly) or $119.99/year. It has 37+ prebuilt consent forms, pre-appointment online forms, client before-photo uploads, unlimited before/after/healed photos, signatures locked after signing, aftercare sending, and PDF download/print/email. It is rated 4.3/5 from 37 ratings (US App Store, fetched Nov 2026) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pmu-forms/id1497270923)
- The PMU Forms developer claims almost all forms were "reviewed by health officials". It also claims about 1,887 artist accounts and about 56,000 forms, and says new-subscriber prices "going up soon" (vendor claims) — [App Store listing via search](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pmu-forms/id1497270923)
- **REV23** (tattoo/body-mod studio software, includes PMU): Private Studio (1-2 artists) $19.99/mo, Small (3-6) $45.99, Medium (7-12) $99.99, Large (13-25) $199.99. All features are on every plan, priced by number of service providers. It has a 14-day trial, no contract and no card required. No yearly price is shown. The forms cover medical history, ID capture, age verification, guardian co-sign and e-signatures — [REV23 pricing](https://www.rev23.com/pricing/); [REV23 forms](https://www.rev23.com/features/forms/)
- **PMU Sign** (Clint Reed, iPad): free on the App Store. It is a form designer plus a companion app for collecting submissions. I found no subscription pricing. Capterra shows 3.0 from a single review — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1485537350?l=en-us); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.ca/software/194761/pmu-sign)
- **Microblading App** (Ana Perrone): iOS is 4.6/5 from 248 ratings. It is free with in-app purchases, including a $2.99 "value pack" with no time limit and a pay-as-you-go option with 10 forms. Android is 2.39/5 from 129 ratings, with a $9.99/month subscription for unlimited forms. Templates are in English, Portuguese and Spanish. These figures come from third-party trackers and conflict between platforms — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1201227310); [AppGoblin (Android)](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.workinprogress.microblading); [AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1201227310)

**UK-origin aesthetics tools (note the GBP pricing and UK/GDPR framing)**
- **Consentz** (UK): from £49/month with a free trial, and quotes by sales. Capterra and Software Advice describe it as "per feature, per month". An aggregator says costs scale to several hundred pounds a month for multi-practitioner clinics. It is rated 4.3 on Capterra (17 reviews). On Software Advice, value for money scores 4.06 against 4.35 for ease of use — [Consentz](https://www.consentz.com/?p=17089); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/171328/Consentz/); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/medical/consentz-profile/reviews/); [Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/consentz)
- **Faces Consent** (UK): free app, and the vendor claims "no subscription fee". It has 100+ consent and aftercare templates (2024 press release), and the forms are oriented to injectables (Botox, filler, PDO, Profhilo). Competitor Pabau claims Faces stores forms as PDFs that don't sync to records, which is a competitor claim. The vendor says it is GDPR compliant, which is not a US/HIPAA framing — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/faces-consent/id1467896114); [press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/london-news-hub/pressreleases/faces-introduces-comprehensive-solutions-for-aesthetic-businesses-3331399); [Pabau compare](https://pabau.com/compare/faces-consent/)
- **Pabau** (UK, sold in the US): from $62/month (Starter, 1 user, up to 100 patients), with higher tiers by quote. Annual billing saves up to 20% (Pabau's own blog, prices checked Sept 2026). Capterra lists from US$59, flat rate, with no free trial or free version — [Pabau blog](https://pabau.com/blog/best-aesthetic-clinic-software/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140062/Pabau-CRM/reviews/?page=2)

**Booking suites with forms**
- **GlossGenius** (US): Standard is $28/mo ($24 annual) and does not list forms. "Forms & Waivers" requires Gold at $56/mo ($48 annual) or above. Medspa plans cost $148-$298/mo. HIPAA is a free add-on. Processing is 2.4% + $0.15 in person and 2.9% + $0.30 for invoices (fetched Nov 2026) — [GlossGenius pricing](https://glossgenius.com/pricing)
- **Vagaro** (US): from $23.99/mo for one calendar, plus $10/mo per extra calendar. Forms are a paid add-on, which multiple third-party sources put at about $10/mo. One pricing table lists forms as included, so the sources conflict — [Pabau blog, Sept 2026](https://pabau.com/blog/best-aesthetic-clinic-software/); [GlossGenius blog (competitor)](https://glossgenius.com/blog/vagaro-pricing-a-complete-breakdown-of-costs); [Launchspace Dec 2025](https://launchspace.net/product/vagaro-pricing-overview/)
- **Square Appointments** (US): a free plan for one location and one staff member. Plus is given as $49/mo by Koalendar and FrontDeskReview but $29 by Costbench. Costbench reports the top tier was cut from $149 to $69 on Feb 4, 2026. No source tied intake forms to a specific tier. GetApp lists "electronic forms" as a feature. Processing is 2.4-3.3% — [Koalendar](https://koalendar.com/blog/square-appointments-pricing); [Costbench changelog](https://costbench.com/changelog/square-appointments-price-decrease-2026-02/); [FrontDeskReview](https://frontdeskreview.com/online-booking/vendors/square-appointments/); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/customer-management-software/a/square-appointments/)
- A third-party vendor (EasyDocForms) sells HIPAA-claimed consent/intake forms to Square-using solo estheticians at $25/mo. Its existence suggests Square's built-in forms leave a gap — [EasyDocForms](https://easydocforms.com/blog/esthetician-consent-forms-square/)
- **Fresha** (UK-origin, global): the sources conflict on whether a free plan still exists. Pabau (a competitor) says paid tiers began in 2025 at about $14.95 per team member per month, with a solo "Independent" plan at $19.95. Costbench and SchedulingKit still list a free plan. There is a 20% new-client fee on marketplace bookings (minimum $6) and processing of about 2.19% + $0.20. Competitors call its consent forms generic — [Pabau Fresha pricing](https://pabau.com/blog/fresha-pricing/); [Costbench](https://costbench.com/software/salon-spa/fresha/); [Fresha blog](https://www.fresha.com/blog/great-news-were-removing-online-booking-fees-at-fresha)

**Generic form builders with PMU templates**
- Jotform, Typeform, Paperform, forms.app and Zoho all publish free PMU or microblading consent templates — [Typeform](https://www.typeform.com/templates/permanent-makeup-consent-form); [Paperform](https://paperform.co/templates/permanent-makeup-consent-form/); [forms.app](https://forms.app/en/templates/permanent-makeup-consent-form); [Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/forms/templates/consent-forms/esthetician-consent-form.html)
- Jotform: paid plans start at about $34/mo (annual). Its HIPAA features (encryption, audit, PHI segmentation) come only with Gold at $99/mo annual ($129 monthly) or Enterprise — [Activepieces 2026](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/jotform-pricing-2026-plans-costs-what-you-get)
- Typeform Basic is $28/mo for 100 responses. Sources conflict on whether HIPAA is on Business or Enterprise. Paperform starts at $24/mo annual, and I found no HIPAA information for it — [Paperform comparison (vendor-biased)](https://paperform.co/form-builders/paperform-vs-jotform-vs-typeform/)
- The Paperform template notes that consent is required by law in most countries, so local rules should be checked — [Paperform](https://paperform.co/templates/permanent-makeup-consent-form/)

### Inferences
- A US solo PMU artist's realistic cheapest digital paths are PMU Forms (~$120/yr), REV23 (~$240/yr) or the Microblading App (pay-per-form or $9.99/mo). Getting forms through a booking suite costs ~$576/yr (GlossGenius Gold annual) or Vagaro plus the forms add-on (~$408/yr). HIPAA-grade Jotform costs ~$1,188/yr.
- Several tools (Consentz, Faces, Pabau, Fresha) come from the UK and show it in GBP pricing, GDPR framing and injectable-centric templates. That is a mismatch for US PMU wording and state body-art rules.

### Gaps
- I found no official Vagaro or Square pages confirming forms pricing or tiers. The figures above are third-party.
- PMU Sign's monetization is unknown.
- The PMU Forms "Standard" billing period is not explicitly stated on the listing.

## What reviewers praise and complain about

### Takeaway
Praise centers on fast setup, pre-appointment online forms, signatures locked on file, photo storage and smooth minor/guardian consent. Complaints center on reliability at the worst moment (loading failures before appointments, lost access to client records), opaque or stacking costs (add-ons, per-feature pricing, marketplace fees), weak support, and generic or non-clinical templates. Review counts for PMU-specific tools are very small.

### Cited Findings
- PMU Forms praise: setup in under five minutes, with "Beautiful app designs and easy to use" (May 2025). Another reviewer wrote "Way better now!" after a redesign (Nov 2025) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pmu-forms/id1497270923)
- PMU Forms complaint: it kept buffering and lost client data access before an appointment, and "stops working of course when I need it most" (Oct 2024). Another user lost access to stored client info and couldn't find support (Jan 2023). The developer replied that server capacity had been doubled — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pmu-forms/id1497270923)
- REV23: 13 US App Store written reviews, about 92% positive (small sample). Praise covers the smooth minor sign-in and ID scanning. One reviewer could not find PDF export, and the developer said PDFs exist (a discoverability problem) — [AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/rev23/id1530843532/reviews)
- Faces Consent: an App Store review reports slow loading and functions that "never work", and the user abandoned the app — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/faces-consent/id1467896114)
- Microblading App: rated well on iOS (4.6) but poorly on Android (2.39), a sign of platform-inconsistent quality — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.workinprogress.microblading)
- PMU Sign: one testimonial says going digital streamlined their process and kept client info safe — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1485537350?l=en-us)
- Pabau: Capterra pros/cons summaries flag "frequent technical glitches and instability" and an "unclear and costly pricing structure". The rating is still about 4.6-4.7 from about 620-640 reviews, and onboarding is praised. Pabau's own blog admits there is no low-cost starter plan for brand-new practices and a learning curve — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140062/Pabau-CRM/reviews/Capterra___3582614/); [Pabau blog](https://pabau.com/blog/best-aesthetic-clinic-software/)
- Consentz: value for money scores lowest of its rated attributes (4.06) — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/medical/consentz-profile/reviews/)
- Vagaro: add-on fees "stack up", and clinical charting is thin (per competitor Pabau) — [Pabau blog](https://pabau.com/blog/best-aesthetic-clinic-software/)
- Fresha: the main complaints are the 20% marketplace new-client fee (including on clients the salon brought in itself), removal of the free plan, and slow support in fee disputes (Trustpilot/ProductReview, individual accounts). One Medium post by a user defends the fees as transparent — [Pabau Fresha reviews](https://pabau.com/blog/fresha-reviews/); [Trustpilot](https://fi.trustpilot.com/review/fresha.com); [Medium](https://medium.com/@asbaines/the-true-cost-of-fresha-276d6856bcc0)

### Inferences
- The biggest risk reviewers raise is the app failing when the client is already in the chair. A printed backup form neutralizes that risk.
- "Subscription creep" shows up as forms locked behind higher tiers (GlossGenius Gold), forms sold as add-ons (Vagaro) and HIPAA locked behind top tiers (Jotform).
- Branding/customization and US-vs-UK wording complaints were not directly found in reviews. They are inferred from template sources and UK vendor positioning.

### Gaps
- Capterra US pages for Consentz returned 403, so I could not read individual reviews.
- I found no direct reviews mentioning lock-in or export difficulty, apart from REV23's PDF confusion.
- HIPAA worries from artists themselves were not found. Only vendors raise HIPAA.

## Do artists still use paper, and how do they store records?

### Takeaway
Paper remains common in PMU, but I could not gather artist-voiced evidence on why, because Reddit was inaccessible. Educators and vendors push digital. Many US states require signed consent and record retention for body art (often covering PMU), with retention periods of 1-7 years that vary by state. Sources conflict on the exact years.

### Cited Findings
- A Tina Davies (PMU brand/educator) guide says PMU artists have traditionally used paper consent forms. It recommends collecting information digitally before the appointment and suggests Google Forms as the simplest option. It also advises adapting wording to local guidelines — [Tina Davies guide](https://tinadavies.com/pages/the-essential-guide-to-pmu-consent-forms); [Tina Davies blog](https://tinadavies.com/blogs/news/what-to-include-in-your-pmu-consent-forms)
- Printed PMU consent form packs are sold on Etsy, which shows a paid market for printable forms exists — [Etsy listing](https://beautech.patternbyetsy.com/listing/4327697675/permanent-makeup-forms-pmu-consent-form)
- Oklahoma requires signed consent forms for tattoo procedures and treats client records as confidential — [OK Admin Code 310:233-3-8](https://regulations.justia.com/states/oklahoma/title-310/chapter-233/subchapter-3/section-310-233-3-8)
- State rules on body art records exist in Utah, Georgia and Delaware, among others — [Utah R392-701-14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R392-701-14); [Georgia 511-3-8-.08](https://regulations.justia.com/states/georgia/department-511/chapter-511-3/subject-511-3-8/rule-511-3-8-08); [Delaware 4451-6.0](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/delaware/16-Del-Admin-Code-SS-4451-6.0)
- Retention periods by state come from vendor guides, which conflict with each other. One guide says California 3 years, Texas 5, New York unspecified. Another says NY 1, TX/HI 2, MN/AK/NM 3, KS/NE 5, NH 7. Both are vendor blogs and are unverified — [TattooStudioPro state guide](https://tattoostudiopro.pages.dev/state-by-state-compliance-guide-for-digital-consent-forms/); [TattooStudioPro checklist](https://tattoostudiopro.pages.dev/checklist-for-tattoo-studio-consent-compliance/)
- Vendor guidance says storage can be a locked file cabinet or an encrypted digital system, forms should be locked after submission, and liability insurers may set their own retention requirements — [TattooStudioPro checklist](https://tattoostudiopro.pages.dev/checklist-for-tattoo-studio-consent-compliance/)
- The NEHA Body Art Model Code is a reference model that many state and local rules draw on — [NEHA BAMC](https://pubs.neha.org/view/461230999/22/)

### Inferences
- Because regulators accept paper (a locked cabinet), paper is a legitimate compliant option. A printed kit should say clearly how long to keep forms and how to store them.
- A kit should frame retention as "check your state; commonly 2-7 years" rather than give one number.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or forum evidence on why artists stay on paper (cost, simplicity, client preference, inspector preference). This is the largest gap, and manual Reddit review is recommended (r/PermanentMakeup, r/microblading, r/Esthetics).
- No primary-source verification of the state retention periods.

## Gaps for brand-new solo PMU artists

### Takeaway
Tools assume an operating business. They charge monthly from day one, and the affordable ones offer generic or UK/injectable-focused templates. None of the tools reviewed offers business-setup guidance (which forms are needed, state rules, retention, aftercare, pricing). PMU-specific apps are cheap but fragile. Robust suites are overkill and expensive before the first client.

### Cited Findings
- Pabau's own comparison says it has no low-cost starter plan for a brand-new practice and takes time to learn — [Pabau blog](https://pabau.com/blog/best-aesthetic-clinic-software/)
- Forms in GlossGenius require the $48-56/mo Gold tier, not the $24-28 entry tier — [GlossGenius pricing](https://glossgenius.com/pricing)
- PMU Forms bundles 37+ prebuilt forms and fast setup at the lowest cost (~$120/yr), but has reliability complaints — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pmu-forms/id1497270923)
- Faces Consent is free, but its templates target injectables (Botox, filler, PDO) and it frames compliance under GDPR — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/faces-consent/id1467896114)
- Generic builders offer free templates, but templates warn to check local rules and HIPAA costs $99+/mo on Jotform — [Paperform](https://paperform.co/templates/permanent-makeup-consent-form/); [Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/jotform-pricing-2026-plans-costs-what-you-get)

### Inferences
- A one-time, US-worded, branded printable/PDF kit fits next to subscriptions in three ways:
  1. Before the first client, it costs no recurring fee.
  2. It is a reliable paper backup when the app fails.
  3. It is a "source of truth" form set the artist can later paste into Jotform, Google Forms or a booking suite.
- Features that matter, based on what reviewers value:
  - fillable PDFs with a signature field, or e-sign compatibility
  - pre-appointment intake (medical contraindications)
  - photo/model release
  - minor/guardian consent
  - aftercare sheets
  - brand customization
  - storage and retention guidance
  - US/state-aware wording notes
- Native digital signing with storage is the main thing a PDF kit cannot match. The kit could pair with free signing (fillable PDF plus a free e-sign tool or Google Forms) rather than compete.

### Gaps
- No direct artist testimony on setup effort or "cost before first client". The inferences rest on pricing structures and vendor admissions.
- No data on how many new US PMU artists use paper versus apps.
